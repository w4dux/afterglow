# Afterglow audit, 2026-11-09 (Phase 0)

The audit ran on the owner's Windows 11 PC (Spotify Microsoft Store 1.306, 75 Hz monitor) against the folder `afterglow-stage` as found. No code was changed. Raw measurement files are outside the project, in the session scratchpad. They can be regenerated with the method described in each section.

**Short version.** On this PC the sync clock follows Spotify's reported position almost exactly: within 1 ms at p95 over 4 minutes of normal playback. "Some songs are out of sync" is therefore mostly a lyrics-data problem. Spotify reports every song as **exactly 5.0 s shorter** than its real length. Afterglow uses that length to choose between community uploads, and those uploads disagree by 0.3–1.8 s for some songs. There are also two engine problems you can see:

- Every pause and resume moves the lyrics back about 0.3 s and then forward again. This is a regression from 2.0.3.
- The optional estimated word sweep crawls across instrumental breaks.

## 4. Why some songs feel out of sync: lyric choice test

I ran Afterglow's own LRCLIB selection code (`lrclibProvider` + `chooseHit`, bundled unchanged) on 22 real songs the owner listens to (names left out here), twice: once with the real length and once with Spotify's length (5 s shorter). Each chosen upload was then compared with all other uploads of the same recording (same majority length), as the median line-start difference.

One song was left out of the counts below: my search matched a different recording.

- With the 5-s-short length, **14 of 21 songs** get a **different upload** than with the real length. For most of them the timing difference is small (under 100 ms).
- **Real word-by-word timing is lost:**
  - With the real length, 13 of 21 songs get a word-timed LRCLIB upload; with Spotify's length, only 4 do.
  - The cause is the length penalty (98 → 89). It outweighs the +8 word-timing bonus when a line-timed upload with an odd length matches the short length.
- Several of the uploads chosen with Spotify's length disagree with most other uploads:

| Song | Upload chosen with Spotify's length vs the other uploads |
|---|---|
| Song A | **−1.82 s** (18 other uploads) |
| Song B | **+0.41 s** (17) |
| Song C | **−0.35 s** (16; picks a 201-s upload over the 205-s majority) |
| Song D | first line 3.5 s earlier than the true-length pick (different intro handling) |

- With the 5 s offset, LRCLIB's "consensus among ≥3 uploads" check never runs, because it requires a length within 3 s.
- Every saved copy gets a misleading note: "this version is 5 s longer; check the recording; an offset may not correct this version". 20 of 110 saved copies in a real library carry it.

This is a comparison between uploads, not against the audio, so it shows which songs are at risk rather than proving which upload is right. It does explain "most songs are fine, some are off".

## 6. Findings (new), most important first

**F1. High: Spotify's 5-s-short song length skews lyric choice.**
- Code: `SystemMediaAdapter.ts:132,210` (length = `endMs − startMs`) feeds `match.ts:71-79` (length similarity), `match.ts:87` (reject > 15 s), `LyricsResolver.ts:61` (consensus needs ≤ 3 s), `LyricsResolver.ts:289-290` (note), and `electron/lrclib.ts:32-36` (`/get` with ±2 s server tolerance).
- Effect (section 4):
  - A different upload for 14 of 21 songs, several of them 0.35–1.8 s off the majority.
  - Real word timing for only 4 of 21 songs instead of 13.
- Proposed fix:
  - Learn the offset per player from measurements: the median of "majority upload length − reported length" over recent songs, used only when at least 3 songs agree within ±0.5 s.
  - Until it is learned, score Spotify lengths over a window of [reported − 2 s, reported + 13 s].
  - Prefer the timing consensus among uploads of the majority length.
  - Remove the false "5 s longer" note.
  - Show the learned offset in diagnostics.
  - Unit tests built from today's 22-song data.

**F2. High (regression): pause and resume make the lyrics jump about 0.3 s back, then forward.**
- Code: `SyncEngine.ts:259-263` applies the held pause report after 1.5 s. `SyncEngine.ts:445-450` snaps back on a lagging *playing* report that arrives 3 ms before the pause.
- Measured: both orders happened in two pauses; Spotify's resume report confirmed the un-jumped estimate (within 23–42 ms).
- Proposed fix:
  - Keep the frozen estimate until resume.
  - Treat a backward correction of ≤ 700 ms that is followed within 150 ms by a pause as part of the pause.
  - Turn today's two traces into regression tests and update the test that asserts the 1.5 s release.

**F3. Medium (code path, not observed today): a new song can be searched with the previous song's length.**
- Code: `SystemMediaAdapter.ts:132,174-183` take the length from the first state with the new title, even when the adapter itself decides that state's timeline is stale. `controller.ts:233-236` reloads only when the earlier length was *missing*.
- With the new 15 s hard reject, a song that follows a much longer or shorter song can lose every correct candidate.
- Proposed fix: send no length while `transitionTimeline` is set, and reload lyrics when the length changes by more than 2 s.

**F4. Medium: the estimated word sweep crawls across instrumental breaks.**
- Code: `scene.ts:268-270`. The sweep span is at least 40% of the time to the next line, so a short line before a 20 s break is swept over 8 s or more. The line also stays highlighted through the break (item 3).
- The owner has the estimated sweep **on**.
- Proposed fix: size the span from the song's pace only (for example syllables ÷ pace × 1.3, at most about 6 s), and add estimated line ends.

**F5. Low: up to 174 ms ahead for about 1.5 s after a seek.**
- Code: `SystemMediaAdapter.ts:276-288` extrapolates the optimistic seek immediately; Spotify stalls about 0.2 s.
- Proposed fix: for Spotify, start the optimistic position about 150 ms late, or hold it until the first confirming report (within the existing 2.5 s timeout).

**F6. Medium: saved lyrics are re-searched online on every play and can be swapped mid-song.**
- Code: `LyricsResolver.ts:228-237,256-269,281-296`; `controller.ts:292-298`.
- Also raises LRCLIB load: this audit hit LRCLIB's **429 rate limit** after a few dozen requests.
- Proposed fix:
  - Recheck at most weekly (item 5).
  - Keep the shown copy for the current play unless the new one has real word timing and no line has started yet.
  - Back off on 429.
  - Remember "not found" (item 6).

**F7. Low: the saved lyrics and the AMLL index live inside Chromium's `Cache` folder.**
- Code: `lyricsCache.ts:13`, `amll.ts:33` use `userData/cache/...`. On Windows that is the same folder as Chromium's `Cache` (`Cache/Cache_Data`).
- Cleaning tools treat "Cache" as disposable, so the offline copies can vanish.
- Proposed fix: move to `userData/library/` with a one-time move of existing files.

**F8. Low: the seek bar shows Spotify's shortened length.**
- 4:41 instead of 4:47 for Orbiter. Fixed together with F1 once the offset is learned (display only).

**F9. Low: the "version tag" hard reject is broad.**
- Code: `match.ts:88-89`. "clean", "live" and "radio edit" in only one of the two titles scores 0, so a Spotify "Radio Edit" can lose LRCLIB's untagged upload of the same audio.
- Proposed fix: reduce the score instead of rejecting, and keep the length check as the real guard.

**Security.** Sandbox, context isolation, no Node in the renderer, CSP in the built HTML, blocked navigation and new windows, and an allow-listed `openExternal` are all in place.
- `lyrics:read` reads any `.lrc/.txt/.xml/.ttml` path the renderer asks for (low risk; the renderer is sandboxed).
- `bridge:command` passes objects through without validation; the Rust side does validate the command name (low).
- No new issues from the owner's changes.

**Performance.** No per-frame React renders were found. The loop stops when paused. Scene cost is 0.13–0.25 ms on average per frame.

**Beginner experience.** There is no first-run flow (item 1). Following Spotify needs a trip to Sources. Notes like F1's "check the recording" sound like errors to a beginner.
